A five-year plan for building something that matters

I am finishing a PhD on how artificial intelligence restructures work: what changes inside jobs as automation takes hold, and what it means for workers navigating those changes. The degree completes later this year, which makes this the right moment to think deliberately about what comes next.

This document describes two things I want to build at CoBLTech: a research programme on AI and work (LOOM), and a community of practice that lifts the research capacity of the college (the CoBLTech Accelerator). Both need institutional support to reach their potential, and both create something of genuine value for the college.

What follows is the plan, what it asks of you, and what it offers in return. I'm sharing it now because the CoBLTech spotlight creates a real opening, and I want to take full advantage of the opportunity.

What I am building

Two things that reinforce each other

The plan is not a single research program. It is a research program and an institutional contribution, designed to grow together and to be stronger for it.

Research program

LOOM

Labour, Occupations, Organisations and Machines. A program studying what AI does to work: how labour demand shifts within occupations, which jobs disappear, and what it takes for workers to move between them. Built on 9.5 million job advertisements, a directed-search equilibrium model, and four live research platforms.

  • 9 papers targeted across labour economics, management, information systems, and SoTL
  • 4 AI platforms as live research instruments, generating longitudinal data unavailable to survey-based research
  • ARC Linkage application in development for 2029
  • Positions RMIT as Australia's leading voice on AI and within-occupation task reallocation
Institutional contribution

CoBLTech Accelerator

An offer to colleagues: if you have an idea for something exciting in the classroom or a question about AI and your students, but need the tools, infrastructure, or technical capability to actually do it, I can help build that. You bring the question. I'll help make it real.

  • Training workshops: using AI to build educational resources, game-based learning, coaching personas, and behavioural experiments
  • Custom platform builds: tools designed around your specific teaching or research context
  • Platform access: deploy Augmenti, Docilis, or Passage as live instruments in your own course or project
  • Annual AI & Work Showcase: CoBLTech as Australia's host for the AI and future of work conversation

Where colleagues use platforms or data I've built for their research, I contribute to study design and co-author the output.

What I need from you

Six things that would make this real

Each of these is something I'd welcome a conversation about. I've tried to be specific about what I'm looking for and why it matters, but I'm open to how that looks in practice.

01

Named CoBLTech affiliation for LOOM

Formal recognition of LOOM as a CoBLTech research program: a named home that appears in my publications, grant applications, and public profile.

For CoBLTech: every LOOM publication and media mention carries the college's name into labour economics, management, and information systems.

02

A governance or committee role

A meaningful seat at a college or university committee: AI integration, curriculum, or equivalent. Not a box-tick, a genuine contribution.

For CoBLTech: someone with deep AI expertise who can bring that knowledge to institutional decisions, not just publish about it.

03

Protected research time post-PhD

Confirmation that the 15% protected research time in my continuing role is maintained through at least 2028, the period in which the Level C case is built.

For CoBLTech: the platforms, publications, and grant income only materialise if the research time is there to produce them.

04

IP and licensing support for Ordian

A conversation with the relevant parties about RMIT's preferred IP arrangement for the Ordian platform, currently undefined. A resolved structure enables responsible scale-up.

For CoBLTech: a commercial platform with a clear IP structure generates research income, real-world data, and reputational capital for the college.

05

Government partner introductions

Brokered introductions to Jobs & Skills Australia, the Productivity Commission, or equivalent bodies for the ARC Linkage application, connections the college has that I do not yet.

For CoBLTech: RMIT at the table where national AI and labour policy is made, with an RMIT researcher as the named expert.

06

Hosting the Annual AI & Work Showcase

CoBLTech as the institutional host of an annual event bringing together researchers, practitioners, government, and media around AI and the future of work.

For CoBLTech: a recurring, high-profile event that positions the college as Australia's home for this conversation, and a natural venue for the external partnerships the ARC Linkage requires.

In closing

The timeline is aggressive because the field is still forming. There is a genuine first-mover opportunity for RMIT in the AI and work space, and the window for taking it will not stay open indefinitely.

I would welcome a conversation about any part of this: what looks strong, what needs work, and what the college is in a position to commit to.
