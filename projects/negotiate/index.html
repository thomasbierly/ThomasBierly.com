Project

NEGOTIATE

Negotiation-based Evaluation through Grounded, Oral Tasks for Integrity, Authenticity and Transferable Education

A 30-minute AI-mediated oral defence where students defend their own work to a lifelike executive avatar — challenged on their specific claims, scored against a formal rubric, and built to assess what written work cannot.

In Development — S2 2026 Pilot

The Problem

Oral defences are among the most effective forms of assessment available to universities. They test whether students actually understand their work — not whether they can produce it, or whether someone else produced it for them. A student who cannot explain what they submitted, or defend the assumptions it rests on, has revealed something important about their learning that no marking rubric will catch.

The problem is scale. Running a genuine 30-minute oral defence with 120 students requires 60 hours of tutor time — before preparation, before marking, before the inevitable rescheduling. Most courses cannot afford it. The ones that try must cut the time per student, which degrades the quality. And inconsistency is structural: different examiners probe different things, at different depths, with different follow-up, which means two students submitting identical work can have very different assessment experiences.

NEGOTIATE is built on the premise that AI can solve the scale and consistency problems without giving up the rigor. The same senior executive challenges every student, with equal intensity, on their specific work. The interview takes 30 minutes. It runs in parallel. The tutor's role shifts from examiner to reviewer — confirming AI-generated scores, reading transcripts, exercising academic judgment where it matters.

The Student Experience

Each student uploads their written assignment and enters a 30-minute live session with a facilitator-assigned executive persona. The executive avatar opens with a challenge drawn directly from the student's submission — not a generic question about the topic, but a question about what the student wrote: their specific financial projections, their implementation timeline, their ethical framing, their assumptions about stakeholder behaviour.

  1. Select an executive. The facilitator configures which of the four AI-powered personas are available for the session — one or all four. Each has a distinct seniority, specialisation, and set of concerns. The CFO will interrogate the numbers. The Head of People will push on workforce impact. The COO will probe operational feasibility. The CIO will challenge data governance.
  2. Upload the assignment. The system reads the submission and extracts a structured negotiation brief — the specific claims, timelines, assumptions, and ethical framings the executive will hold the student to during the session.
  3. Enter the defence. The executive opens with a challenge. The student responds by voice. The avatar replies in real time — lip-synced, naturally voiced, and with full context of both the student's written work and everything said so far in the session.
  4. Receive a grade. After the session, the transcript is scored against a five-criterion rubric. The facilitator reviews the AI-generated score, reads the transcript, and confirms or adjusts before releasing results to the student.
The Core Innovation
The avatar doesn't just know the topic. It knows your work.

A generic AI oral examiner would ask the same questions to everyone. NEGOTIATE extracts a structured brief from each student's submission before the session begins — key claims, challengeable assumptions, specific commitments — and injects it into every prompt. When the executive pushes back, it is pushing back on what this student actually argued. Students who submitted vague claims cannot hide behind generality. Students who made strong, specific arguments are challenged at the level of detail those arguments deserve.

The Assessment Rubric

The session is scored across five criteria, each rated on a four-point scale. The rubric is designed to capture what an oral defence specifically tests — not the quality of the written work, which is assessed separately, but the student's ability to think under pressure, respond to challenge, and demonstrate genuine understanding of what they submitted.

Criterion P — Pass HD — High Distinction
Command of analysis Defends general claims accurately; acknowledges challenges when raised Defends beyond the submission; anticipates follow-up challenges before they are raised
Evidence & evaluation Cites evidence when challenged; broadly supports claims Cites specific sources; distinguishes between evidence and inference; concedes gracefully when warranted
Strategic & ethical reasoning Identifies trade-offs when prompted; holds core position under pressure Identifies trade-offs explicitly; holds core position while adjusting periphery; generates new options under pressure
Stakeholder responsiveness Addresses concerns directly; maintains appropriate framing for the executive Addresses specific concerns rather than generic rebuttals; tunes framing to the executive's professional lens; builds rapport
Oral presentation Generally clear; some hesitation under pressure but maintains coherence Concise and structured; manages pace and silence; recovers from difficulty fluidly

This component is worth 30% of the course grade. AI-generated scores are research instruments only — facilitators review every transcript and confirm or override before grades are released to students.

Technical Architecture

The system orchestrates five distinct AI and cloud services within a single 30-minute session: speech recognition, large language model inference, neural text-to-speech with phoneme-level timing, real-time 3D avatar rendering with lip-sync, and composite video recording. Each service is chosen for a specific reason, and the seams between them are where the interesting engineering lives.

  • Frontend React 18 + Vite + React Three Fiber (Three.js) — 3D avatar rendered in WebGL, UI in Tailwind CSS
  • 3D Avatar ReadyPlayerMe GLB + morph target visemes — Polly Speech Marks drive per-phoneme lip animation without offline processing
  • Interview AI Anthropic Claude Sonnet 4.6 — reads student's negotiation brief + full conversation history; generates each executive response
  • Brief extraction Anthropic Claude Sonnet 4.6 — parses uploaded PDF/DOCX into structured brief: key claims, timelines, assumptions, ethical framings
  • Grading AI Anthropic Claude Opus 4.8 — scores full transcript against the five-criterion rubric post-session; generates per-criterion feedback
  • Speech recognition Amazon Transcribe Streaming (real-time) — captures student voice continuously during the session
  • Text-to-speech Amazon Polly Neural (Australian voices: Olivia/Russell) — synthesises avatar audio + returns JSON Speech Marks for lip-sync timing
  • Recording Browser MediaRecorder + offscreen Canvas composite — avatar and student webcam (PiP) captured as WebM without screen recording
  • Backend Node.js 20 + Express + Prisma — REST API, session management, AI orchestration, JWT authentication
  • Database PostgreSQL on AWS RDS (ap-southeast-2) — session state, message history, transcripts, rubric scores, research event log
  • Storage & hosting AWS S3 (recordings + uploads) + App Runner (backend) + CloudFront (frontend) — all in ap-southeast-2 for RMIT data-residency compliance
Why Polly Speech Marks
Lip-sync without Rhubarb, without FFmpeg, without offline processing.

Most approaches to avatar lip-sync require converting audio to viseme sequences through a separate tool like Rhubarb Lip Sync, which needs FFmpeg, takes time, and adds a processing step between speech synthesis and playback. Amazon Polly's Neural voices return JSON Speech Marks alongside the audio — an array of viseme codes with millisecond timestamps. The avatar maps those codes directly to Wolf3D morph targets and animates in sync with playback. No pipeline, no waiting, no additional dependencies.

Research Design

NEGOTIATE is designed as a formal research project alongside its educational function. The S2 2026 pilot with approximately 120 students across BUSM2675 and BUSM4537 is the first data collection phase, and the system's architecture reflects the research questions it is designed to answer.

Between-subjects comparison
Grounded vs. generic condition
50% of students receive an AI examiner grounded in their specific work. 50% face a pre-authored generic scenario. Allows formal comparison of whether grounding improves performance, fairness, and perceived authenticity.
AI grading validity
AI score vs. human judgment
All sessions are scored by Claude Opus against the rubric. Facilitators grade independently before seeing the AI score. The study examines whether AI and human scores converge, and under what conditions they diverge.
Tutor confidence study
Two-stage attribution analysis
Facilitators rate their confidence in the AI grade before and after reading the student's written submission. Tests whether access to the underlying work changes how evaluators interpret AI assessment.
Immutable event log
Full audit trail for analysis
Every session event — message timestamps, speaking durations, decision timings, grade confirmations — is logged as an immutable research record, enabling post-hoc analysis of engagement patterns.
