Part 7

Make it yours

You now have a computer where the agent can read and change almost everything, including the setup itself. That makes it a place to build the research tools you’ve always wanted, and to share them.

Play, build and share

The setup in this course isn’t finished, and it isn’t meant to be. Every part of it, down to the menus, is text your agent can read and change. That means the most useful software for your research may be software that doesn’t exist yet, and you can ask for it.

Start with “I wish I had a tool that…” and see how far your agent gets. Some ideas:

A small app for coding interview transcripts, with your codebook built in
A page that shows which participants you still need to recruit
A Monday-morning digest of new papers on your topic, straight into Zotero
A script that turns lecture notes into slides and a handout
A checker that flags any number in your paper the analysis doesn’t produce
A new project template for the kind of study you run most

Play with it. Your projects are saved version by version, so a bad experiment is one step to undo, and your agent can usually put back anything it changed on the computer. Most of what’s in this course started as an afternoon of asking Claude for something I wanted.

Built something? Show me.

I’d love to see what researchers make with this: a tool, a template, a better way of working, or something that broke and how you fixed it. The best ideas may end up in the setup for everyone.

When you’re stuck, ask your agent. It can read the error, explain it and usually fix it. Try “the PDF preview isn’t updating, can you find out why?” For Omarchy itself, the Omarchy manual and its Discord are friendly places to ask.
